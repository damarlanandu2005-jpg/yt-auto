## s01 — The Paradox of Time and Distance in the Universe

1. How can the observable universe be 93 billion light-years across if it is only 13.8 billion years old?
2. If light travels at a constant speed, this vast cosmic scale sounds like an impossible contradiction.
3. So why does this cosmic puzzle matter so much when we look out into space?
4. Objects beyond this physical boundary cannot be detected yet because their light has not had enough time to reach Earth.
5. In this video, we will answer three fundamental questions about our cosmic horizon in plain words.
6. How does space stretch while light is actively traveling across the universe?
7. How does a travel time of 13.8 billion years convert into a physical radius of 46.5 billion light-years?
8. And what mysterious regions lie forever beyond the edge of our current observable cosmos?
9. To solve this mystery, we must reframe how we imagine cosmic space and time.

## s02 — The False Picture of Galaxies Flying Through Empty Space

1. Many people visualize the universe as a huge empty room where galaxies rocket through a dark void.
2. In that false mental picture, galaxies are racing through a fixed, static space like cosmic shrapnel.
3. If that picture were correct, nothing could ever appear farther away than 13.8 billion light-years.
4. But this common picture of cosmic expansion is completely wrong.
5. Cosmic expansion is not galaxies moving through a pre-existing empty space.
6. Instead, general relativity describes expansion as an intrinsic increase in distance between cosmic objects over time.
7. Most galaxies are nearly at rest relative to their local comoving spatial coordinates.
8. The velocity of a galaxy relative to its local comoving grid is called its peculiar velocity.
9. These local peculiar velocities are caused mostly by gravity and are usually quite small.
10. So how should we picture the movement of galaxies as the universe expands?
11. Understanding this key distinction changes everything about how we calculate cosmic distances.
