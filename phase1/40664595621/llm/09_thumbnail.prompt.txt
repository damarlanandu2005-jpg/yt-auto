To solve this mystery, we must reframe how we imagine cosmic space and time.
Many people visualize the universe as a huge empty room where galaxies rocket through a dark void.

If that picture were correct, nothing could ever appear farther away than 13.8 billion light-years.
But this common picture of cosmic expansion is completely wrong.
Cosmic expansion is not galaxies moving through a pre-existing empty space.
Instead, general relativity describes expansion as an intrinsic increase in distance between cosmic objects over time.
Most galaxies are nearly at rest relative to their local comoving spatial coordinates.
The velocity of a galaxy relative to its local comoving grid is called its peculiar velocity.
These local peculiar velocities are caused mostly by gravity and are usually quite small.
So how should we picture the movement of galaxies as the universe expands?
