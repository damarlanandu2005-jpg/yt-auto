{{short description|Phenomenon in astrophysics}} {{use mdy dates|date=June 2023}} [[File:Spaghettification (from NASA's Imagine the Universe!).png|alt=|thumb|Astronaut falling into a black hole (schematic illustration of the spaghettification effect){{cn|date=July 2026}}]] In [[astrophysics]], '''spaghettification''',{{cite book|author-link=Nigel Calder|last=Calder|first=Nigel|title=The Key to the Universe: A Report on the New Physics|publisher=[[Viking Press]]|date=1977|isbn=978-0-67041270-9|url=https://archive.org/details/keytouniverserep0000cald/page/143|page=143|url-access=registration|access-date=July 10, 2022}} Published as a companion to the [[BBC]] TV documentary ''The Key to the Universe''. or '''noodle effect'''{{Cite book |last=Wheeler |first=J. Craig |url=https://www.worldcat.org/title/ocm73954922 |title=Cosmic catastrophes: exploding stars, black holes, and mapping the universe |date=2007 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-0-521-85714-7 |edition=2nd |location=Cambridge; New York |pages=182 |oclc=ocm73954922}} is the vertical stretching and horizontal compression of objects into long thin shapes in a very strong, non-[[homogeneous]] [[gravitational field]]. The term was popularized by [[Stephen Hawking]] who described the flight of a fictional [[astronaut]] who, passing within a black hole's [[event horizon]], is "stretched like [[spaghetti]]".{{Cite journal |last=Pinochet |first=Jorge |date=July 1, 2022 |title=The little robot, black holes, and spaghettification |url=https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6552/ac5727 |journal=Physics Education |volume=57 |issue=4 |pages=045008 |doi=10.1088/1361-6552/ac5727 |issn=0031-9120|arxiv=2203.04759 |bibcode=2022PhyEd..57d5008P }}{{cite book|first=Stephen|last=Hawking|author-link=Stephen Hawking|title=[[A Brief History of Time]]|isbn=978-0-553-10953-5|date=1988|publisher=Bantam Dell Publishing Group|pages=256}} It is caused by extreme [[tidal force]]s. In the most extreme cases, near a [[black hole]], the stretching and compression are so powerful that no object can resist it. Within a small region, the horizontal compression balances the vertical stretching so that a small object being spaghettified experiences no net change in volume. If a person were to fall into a black hole feet first, the gravity at their feet would be much stronger than at their head, causing them to be vertically stretched. Along with that, the right side of the body will be pulled to the left, and the left side of the body will be pulled to the right, horizontally compressing the person.{{Cite book |last1=Fraknoi |first1=Andrew |author-link=Andrew Fraknoi |title=Astronomy |last2=Morrison |first2=David |author-link2=David Morrison (astrophysicist) |last3=C. Wolff |publisher=OpenStax |year=2016 |isbn=9781938168284 |pages=862}} ==A simple example== [[File:Spaghettification.gif|thumb|left|The spaghettification of four objects falling towards a planet{{cn|date=July 2026}}]] The concept can be illustrated with four objects in space above a planet, positioned in a diamond formation. The force on each object is directed towards the planet's center. In accordance with the [[inverse-square law]], the lowest of the four objects experiences the biggest gravitational acceleration, and two objects on each side are pulled towards each other, so that the whole formation becomes stretched along the line to the planet and compressed transversely. If these four objects are connected parts of a larger rigid object, then it will resist distortion. Internal elastic forces develop as the body distorts to balance the tidal forces, to maintain [[mechanical equilibrium]]. If the tidal forces become too large, the body may yield creating a narrow line of broken pieces. The forces responsible for spaghettification are called [[tidal forces]]. The radial tidal force across an object of mass {{mvar|m}} and size \Delta r at a distance {{mvar|r}} from an object of mass {{mvar|M}} can be estimated with [[Newton's law of gravity]]: \Delta F = \Delta r\frac{2GmM}{r^3} where {{mvar|G}} is the [[gravitational constant]]. == Relation to the event horizon == [[File:tde-simulation.jpg|thumb|Simulation of spaghettification of a star by a supermassive black hole{{Cite journal | last1 = Price | first1 = Daniel J. | last2 = Liptai | first2 = David | last3 = Mandel | first3 = Ilya | last4 = Shepherd | first4 = Joanna | last5 = Lodato | first5 = Giuseppe | last6 = Levin | first6 = Yuri | title = Eddington Envelopes: The Fate of Stars on Parabolic Orbits Tidally Disrupted by Supermassive Black Holes | journal = The Astrophysical Journal Letters | volume = 971 | issue = 2 | pages = L46 | year = 2024 | doi = 10.3847/2041-8213/ad6862 | doi-access = free | bibcode = 2024ApJ...971L..46P | arxiv = 2404.09381 }}]] The point at which tidal forces destroy an object or kill a person is proportional to the black hole's mass and is distinct from the event horizon. For a [[supermassive black hole]], such as those found at a galaxy's center, this point lies within the [[event horizon]]. As a result, an astronaut may cross the event horizon without noticing any significant tidal forces. Once inside the event horizon, however, falling toward the center becomes inevitable.{{Cite book |last1=Hawley |first1=John Frederick |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=s5MUDAAAQBAJ |title=Foundations of modern cosmology |last2=Holcomb |first2=Katherine A. |date=2005 |publisher=Oxford University Press |isbn=978-0-19-853096-1 |edition=2nd |location=Oxford; New York |pages=253}} Stellar black holes have much higher spacetime curvature at their event horizon, so the tidal forces would cause the astronaut to undergo spaghettification before the event horizon.{{Cite book |last1=Hobson |first1=Michael Paul |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5dryXCWR7EIC |title=General relativity: an introduction for physicists |last2=Efstathiou |first2=Georges |last3=Lasenby |first3=Anthony N. |date=2006 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-0-521-82951-9 |location=Cambridge |pages=265}}{{Cite book |last=Kutner |first=Marc L. |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2QVmiMW0O0MC |title=Astronomy: a physical perspective |date=2003 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-0-521-52927-3 |edition=2nd |location=Cambridge |pages=150}} ==References== {{reflist|40em}} ==External links== {{Wiktionary}} *[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1iJXOUMJpg Neil DeGrasse Tyson: Death by Black Hole (clear explanation of the term)] * {{Cite web |last=Crew |first=Bec |date=2017-03-24 |title=An Encounter With a Neutron Star Would Dwarf Our Entire Nuclear Arsenal |url=https://www.sciencealert.com/if-an-astronaut-got-too-close-to-a-neutron-star-the-explosion-would-dwarf-our-entire-nuclear-arsenal |access-date=2024-10-05 |website=ScienceAlert |language=en-US}} Describes [[Science Channel]] [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NhOVDDiSvMM video] of spaghettification in a [[neutron star]]. {{Black holes}} [[Category:Black holes]] [[Category:Effects of gravity]] [[Category:Metaphors referring to spaghetti]] [[Category:Tidal forces]]
