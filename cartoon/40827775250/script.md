# What happens if you fall into a black hole?

> Style test (owner, 29 Oct). Every spoken fact is from Wikipedia, revisions saved by fetch-pages run 40820593723:
> Spaghettification rev 1376271770 (S), Black hole rev 1382111817 (B). The hook is a question, answered in the video (R2).

## hook {transition=fade}
Imagine you fall into a black hole, [#feet] feet first. [#ask] What would you feel?

## stretch
Gravity pulls much harder on your feet than on your head. [#long] So your body is stretched long and thin. [#squeeze] At the same time, your sides are squeezed inward.
> S: "the gravity at their feet would be much stronger than at their head, causing them to be vertically stretched ... horizontally compressing the person"

## name
Scientists call this [#word] spaghettification. [#hawking] Stephen Hawking made the word famous.
> S: "The term was popularized by Stephen Hawking"

## size
The size of the black hole changes everything. [#small] A small one stretches you apart before you even reach its edge. [#big] A giant one lets you cross the edge without feeling much at all.
> S: "Stellar black holes ... the tidal forces would cause the astronaut to undergo spaghettification before the event horizon"; "For a supermassive black hole ... an astronaut may cross the event horizon without noticing any significant tidal forces"

## outside
A friend watching from far away sees something strange. [#slow] You seem to slow down near the edge. [#red] You look redder and dimmer, [#gone] until you fade from view.
> B: "appear to slow as it approached the event horizon ... any light emitted by the object to appear redder and dimmer ... would fade away until it could no longer be seen"

## trapped {hold=1.5}
Once you cross the edge, there is no way back. [#light] Nothing, not even light, can escape from inside.
> S: "Once inside the event horizon, however, falling toward the center becomes inevitable." B: "Nothing, not even light, can escape from inside the event horizon."
